Jean had never been out of the country. Not once. A literal passport virgin.

She is also a woman who loves ice in her drink. Not ice. Extra ice.

So when the invitation said Cancun, the worry list wrote itself. Don't drink the water. Don't brush your teeth with it. Don't catch Montezuma's revenge three days before a wedding. Don't think too hard about the violence in Cancun a few years back. And underneath all of it, the plain fear of the unknown.

She was nervous. She is also more of an introvert than you would ever guess from talking to her. A week of travel, strangers, and a wedding party is a lot for anyone. For Jean, it was a lot squared.

My side wasn't clean either. I started a new job in August. In facilities management, the first week of November is peak snow prep. It is not a week you take off.

We went anyway.

## Why we went.

Wes.

Wesley is Wayne and Jodie's son. Wayne and Jodie are good friends of ours, and Jodie has been Jean's best friend since high school. Wes is the life of the party. High energy, good vibes only, loves everyone. Almost everyone who meets him loves him right back.

I've gotten close to Wes over the years. I've been one of his professional mentors since he was in college. He also helped me coach a club baseball team for a few years, so I know dugout Wes. The guy you wanted, and needed, to keep the energy loose and the boys primed to play.

No way were we missing Wes's day.

## The water was fine.

Jean did her research. So did I. The resort's water was fully vetted for gringos like us. Ice, drinking water, all of it, safe everywhere on property.

Weeks of worry. Non-issue.

The real stress of a destination wedding isn't the water. It's the wanting. The coordination. Keeping the family dynamics in balance. Everyone wants it perfect, and that want lands on everyone.

Nobody wanted it more than Jodie. She fretted over every detail so the week would be magical for Wes and Audrey.

Pressure on a wedding doesn't come from what can go wrong. It comes from how badly everyone wants it to go right.

## The hugging phase.

Sunday was the welcome party. Jodie told us Wes wanted to give a little speech to thank everyone for coming. He couldn't do it without crying. He was just that happy so many people came.

The day before the wedding, we got pool cabanas. Jean had a mango daiquiri. Jodie had mimosas. Then more mimosas, because they kept bringing them to her.

At one point Jodie came up to Jean in the pool and hugged her. The full hug. The one that only shows up a few mimosas in. She told Jean she loved her and thanked her for coming.

Jean didn't miss a beat. "Oh. We're in the hugging phase."

We laughed hard. Jodie needed that. She had been working so hard to make it perfect, and the day before, it finally looked like everything was lining up. She let off some steam. Earned.

Me? I worked a lot of that trip, prepping for board meetings the following week. Board meetings don't care what time zone you're in.

## Getting ready.

Jean wore a sleeveless pantsuit. Stunning. Elegant.

I wore my light blue suit. Remember the shirt. We'll come back to it.

I watched her and thought about how much I love her. How grateful I am to have found the person who completes me. I love her more every day. Literally every day.

## The wedding.

The venue came together beautifully. Everyone dressed up. Wayne looked great, even in the baseball hat he wore so he wouldn't burn. Hot and sunny. The hat came off before the ceremony. Jodie looked amazing in her dress.

And Wes. Holding a flood of happiness right behind his eyes.

As people gathered, you could feel it. Same energy everywhere. You could literally feel the happiness coming off people.

Wes walked up with his parents, side by side. Their music started the waterworks for a lot of us. Great picks, Wes and Audrey.

Then the bridesmaids and groomsmen. As each groomsman came in, Wes hugged him and said something only Wes could say. A lot of those guys were his high school baseball teammates. I could feel the love he had for every one of them.

One spot was empty. Romo, his third baseman, couldn't make it. He had just moved for a new job and is building a family of his own. Wes totally understood. I'm sure Romo felt the vibes all the way from Cancun.

Then Amber came down the aisle.

Amber is Wes's sister. Always the life of the party. High energy, full of sass, quick-witted, and one of the hardest-working people you will ever meet. As a single mom, she puts her best foot forward every day. Seeing her walk down that aisle, bright, strong, and proud, made every one of us proud of her.

Then Blakely, Amber's daughter, the flower girl. Eight years old and the spitting image of her mother. Same energy. Same sass. An amazing kid. Her best friend is Wayne. She calls him Grumpy. The two of them have something special.

Then Audrey. Stunning in her dress. Smiling the whole way down, wanting this to be perfect for everyone.

And Wes, watching her come.

That's when the tears finally won. The love of his life walking toward him, and he stopped holding it back.

The ceremony ran about fifteen minutes. Perfect timing.

## The dance floor.

Then the party started, and so did the mojitos.

We caught up with Wayne and Jodie's family and with friends we hadn't seen in forever. We laughed. We drank. Luis and Ramon made sure Mr. Jim's mojitos never ran dry.

The dance floor erupted, and Wes and Audrey never left it. They built the playlist for exactly the vibe they wanted, and it never let up. Luis and Ramon kept everyone well lubricated, and more and more people hit the floor.

Wes's cousins, sisters Mags and Katie, both PhDs, came in with their reserved shells on. The shells didn't survive. Bouncing, singing, laughing.

Then Amber found me. "JIM! Shots! You have to do shots with me whenever I say."

Okay, Bam. Jean calls her Bam sometimes, so I do too. I'm in. Because it's a wedding. Because it's Amber, one of the strongest women I know. I'll match you.

Then I hit the floor. Song after song. Jean was taking videos and would not come out. No thanks.

Until "YMCA." Who doesn't dance to "YMCA"? You have to. Plus, it's for Wes. Jean and I danced.

Then House of Pain, "Jump Around," and suddenly it was 1993 and I was back at Club Boston in Fitchburg, a soldier off Fort Devens, wearing a hoop earring and a denim button-down. The light blue shirt was a few buttons lower by then. One button every five mojitos.

Then "Mr. Brightside." If you're a Bills fan, that song gets danced to and sung at full volume. Not optional.

Then fireworks, ordered special for Wes and Audrey. Then last call. Then it was over.

## The day after.

Everyone was happier. More connected. It kept going for days.

We had one last pool day. Jean with her mango daiquiri. Jodie with another mimosa. Nobody was worried about the water.

We all made memories together that will last our lifetimes. And two people we love made a commitment to love each other forever.

Jean came home with her first passport stamp and a phone full of dance floor videos. I came home to snow prep and board meetings, just like I knew I would.

Worth every mojito.

Man. Who doesn't love weddings?

Jim
